Call 911 or local police and tell them where the person is. Florida's law enforcement agency says that contacting police when an older adult seems lost or disoriented may help prevent a Silver Alert.

## Step 1: Decide if it is an emergency

The U.S. Department of Justice guide *When a Loved One Goes Missing* (November 2021) says to dial 911 when someone is in danger or at immediate risk of injury or death, and names two situations that fit:

- a missing elder with cognitive disabilities
- someone missing during adverse weather, such as extreme heat or cold

The same guide says to dial 911 or contact law enforcement right away when the missing person is younger than 21. When there is no immediate physical danger, it points to the local police non-emergency number instead.

## Step 2: If it is a child in a store or other public place

North Dakota's child safety guidance teaches children who get separated from their parents in a public place not to wander around, but to go to a checkout counter, the security office, or the lost and found and tell the person in charge. Staff at those spots are the people a lost child has been taught to look for.

The same guidance tells parents to teach children their full names, addresses, and phone numbers, so an older child may be able to give them to you or to the police. It also teaches children not to get into a car or go anywhere with anyone unless their parents have said it is OK, so a child who will not go with you is following that rule.

## Step 3: Talk to an older adult gently

Many older adults who seem lost have Alzheimer's disease or another dementia. The National Institute on Aging (NIA) gives these do's and don'ts for talking with a person who has Alzheimer's:

**Do:**

- Make eye contact and use the person's name.
- Listen and be open to their concerns.
- Give them extra time to respond.
- Try different words if they do not understand the first time.
- Ask yes-or-no questions, such as "Would you like some tea?" instead of "What would you like to drink?"

**Don't:**

- Speak in an angry or tense tone.
- Interrupt or argue with them.
- Talk about them as if they are not there.
- Ask whether they remember something or someone.

NIA advises caregivers to have a person who wanders carry ID or wear a medical bracelet with their name, address, and the caregiver's phone number, or to label their clothes with a name and phone number. A lost older adult may be carrying this information.

## If they match an alert you saw

Florida's Department of Law Enforcement asks the public, during a Silver Alert, to note the person's description and to call 911 right away if they see the person or vehicle, noting where the person is and, if applicable, the vehicle tag, direction of travel, and location (street, city, and county).

AMBER Alerts are issued by law enforcement for abducted children who meet the alert criteria. The Justice Department's AMBER Alert guide for broadcasters says alerts should include phone numbers the public can use to contact authorities if the suspect or child is seen, and that to give information about a missing child you can call 911 or 1-800-THE-LOST (1-800-843-5678).

## Other numbers

- **Emergency:** 911
- **National Center for Missing & Exploited Children:** 1-800-THE-LOST (1-800-843-5678), listed by USAGov and the AMBER Alert program for missing children
