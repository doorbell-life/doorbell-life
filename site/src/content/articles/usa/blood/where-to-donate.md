At an American Red Cross blood drive or donation center, or at a local community blood center. Use the Red Cross drive finder, the America's Blood Centers zip code search, or AABB's donation site locator to find one near you.

Blood in the U.S. is collected by more than one organization, mainly the American Red Cross and community blood centers. Which one is closest to you depends on where you live, so it is worth searching with more than one tool.

## Three ways to find a place

In a July 10, 2026 joint statement, AABB (the Association for the Advancement of Blood & Biotherapies), America's Blood Centers, and the American Red Cross asked donors to contact one of these to find a local donation site and book an appointment:

| Organization | Online | Phone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AABB | wheretodonateblood.org | 301-907-6977 |
| America's Blood Centers | americasblood.org | 202-393-5725 |
| American Red Cross | redcrossblood.org | 1-800-RED CROSS (1-800-733-2767) |

### American Red Cross

The Red Cross says it supplies about 40% of the nation's blood. On redcrossblood.org, choose "Find a Blood Drive," enter your zip code, and pick a donation type and time. You can also book through the Red Cross Blood Donor App or by calling 1-800-RED CROSS.

### Community blood centers

America's Blood Centers' national inventory page says there are 59 community blood centers in America. Its "Find a Blood Center" page lets you enter your zip code to find your nearest community blood center and book an appointment. It also offers a downloadable map of member centers by state.

### AABB locator

AABB's site, wheretodonateblood.org, is listed in the joint statement as another way to find a local donation site.

## Pick a donation type first

Not every site offers every type of donation, so choose the type when you search. The Red Cross gives these times for the donation itself:

- whole blood: about 8 to 10 minutes
- Power Red (double red cells) or plasma: about 45 minutes
- platelets: about two hours

The Red Cross suggests selecting a donation type when you make an appointment.

## Before you go

- **Book ahead.** Appointments help centers plan. The Red Cross says that if an immediate appointment is not available, scheduling a donation in the days and weeks ahead still helps keep the supply stable.
- **Check eligibility.** See our article on who can donate blood.
- **Bring ID.** The Red Cross accepts a donor card, driver's license, or two other forms of identification.
- **Plan about an hour.** The Red Cross says a whole blood visit takes about an hour from arrival to departure.

## Can't donate? Other ways to help

The Red Cross lists these options for people who are not eligible:

- host a blood drive
- start a virtual blood drive and invite others to donate
- volunteer

## When supply is low

In its July 27, 2026 release, the Red Cross said type O accounts for about 60% of its blood distributions, and urged all eligible donors, especially those with type O blood, to book an appointment. Your local center's needs may differ from national trends, as America's Blood Centers notes, so check its website for current needs.
