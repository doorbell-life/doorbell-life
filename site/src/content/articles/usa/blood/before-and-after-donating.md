Before you give, sleep well, eat a healthy meal, drink extra fluids, and bring a photo ID and your medication list. Afterward, drink extra fluids, skip alcohol and heavy exercise for the rest of the day, and call the blood center if you feel unwell.

The tips below come from the American Red Cross, which says it supplies about 40% of the nation's blood. Other blood centers give similar advice, but each sets some of its own rules, so follow the instructions your center gives you.

## The days before

- **Make an appointment.** Choose the donation type and a time that works for you.
- **Eat iron-rich foods.** The Red Cross suggests red meat, fish, poultry, beans, spinach, iron-fortified cereals, or raisins. Low iron is one of the common reasons people are turned away on the day.
- **Giving platelets?** Do not take aspirin for 2 days before your appointment, the Red Cross says.
- **Check the waiting period since your last donation.** Federal rules limit whole blood donation to no more than once every 8 weeks, and double red cell donation to no more than once every 16 weeks (21 CFR 630.15). The Red Cross lists whole blood every 56 days, Power Red (double red cells) every 112 days, and platelets every 7 days, up to 24 times a year.
- **Feeling sick?** The Red Cross asks you to cancel if you don't feel well, and says you can come back 24 hours after your symptoms pass.

## The night before and the day of

- **Sleep.** Get a good night's sleep.
- **Drink.** The Red Cross recommends an extra 16 oz. of water or another nonalcoholic drink before your appointment.
- **Eat a healthy meal.** Avoid fatty foods like hamburgers, fries, or ice cream.
- **Dress for it.** Wear a shirt with sleeves you can roll up above the elbow.
- **Bring ID.** Federal rules require blood centers to get proof of identity and a postal address where you can be reached for 8 weeks after donating (21 CFR 630.10(g)). The Red Cross accepts a donor card, driver's license, or two other forms of identification.
- **Bring a medication list.** You will be asked about all prescription and over-the-counter medications.

## At the blood center

According to the Red Cross, the whole visit takes about an hour, and a whole blood donation itself takes about 8 to 10 minutes. About a pint is collected.

Before you give, staff check your temperature, pulse, blood pressure, and hemoglobin, then ask health and travel questions in a private interview. Federal rules set minimum standards for this check, including:

- temperature no higher than 99.5 °F (37.5 °C)
- a regular pulse between 50 and 100 beats per minute (a physician can make exceptions)
- hemoglobin of at least 12.5 g/dL for female donors and 13.0 g/dL for male donors (with a narrow exception for female donors at some centers)
- weight of at least 110 pounds (50 kg)

(21 CFR 630.10(f))

The Red Cross now uses automated devices for some of these checks and offers tips for accurate readings: skip caffeine, warm your hands, sit quietly with your arm on the table at heart level, and keep your feet flat on the floor.

### Feeling faint?

The Red Cross recommends **applied muscle tension**: cross your legs, squeeze your inner thighs or knees together, tighten your stomach muscles, and point and flex your feet. Hold for 5 seconds, relax for 5 seconds, and repeat. It suggests doing this before the needle goes in, during the donation if you feel lightheaded, before the needle comes out, and before you stand up. The Red Cross says the technique reduces the chance of fainting or near-fainting by up to 83%.

## Right after

- **Rest and snack.** Stay in the refreshment area for a few minutes. The Red Cross says most donors leave after about 15 minutes.
- **Drink more.** The Red Cross recommends an extra four 8 oz. glasses of liquids and no alcohol for the next 24 hours.

## The rest of the day

- **Keep the bandage on** for the next several hours. Clean around it with soap and water to avoid a rash.
- **No heavy lifting or vigorous exercise** for the rest of the day.
- **If the needle site bleeds**, press on it and raise your arm straight up for 5 to 10 minutes, or until it stops.
- **If you feel dizzy**, stop, and sit or lie down until you feel better. The Red Cross says to avoid any activity where fainting could cause injury for at least 24 hours.

## The weeks after

- **Keep eating iron-rich foods.** If you donate often, the Red Cross suggests a multivitamin with iron.
- **Call the center if something comes up.** If you remember health information you forgot to share, have a problem, or need medical care after giving, tell your blood center. The Red Cross number is 1-866-236-3276.

Before you donate, federal rules require the center to tell you that a sample of your blood will be tested for specified infections, and that if your donation is found unsuitable or you are deferred, you will be notified of the reason and how long the deferral lasts (21 CFR 630.10(g)(2)).
