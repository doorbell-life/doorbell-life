First keep yourself and the animal safe, then look for a tag, get the animal scanned for a microchip at a vet or shelter, and report it to your local animal services agency the same day; whether you may keep it at home while you look for the owner depends on local rules.

In the United States, found-animal rules are set by cities and counties. This article uses two public agencies as examples, the City of Los Angeles (LA Animal Services) and Montgomery County, Maryland. Your local agency's rules may be different, so look up its "found pet" page.

## 1. Keep everyone safe

A scared animal can bite or run into traffic. Approach slowly and don't chase it; Montgomery County gives the same advice to people searching for a lost pet. If you take the animal home, LA Animal Services advises keeping it safe and separate from your own pets until you know more about its health and behavior.

## 2. Look for a tag

Check the collar for an ID tag, a rabies tag, or a city or county license tag.

- A personal ID tag usually has the owner's phone number. Call it.
- A license tag has a number that the issuing agency can look up. LA Animal Services says people who find a pet with its tag can call the phone number printed on the tag, and the department can identify the owner from the tag number. Montgomery County calls a visible license tag "the fastest way" to reunite a lost pet with its owner.

## 3. Get it scanned for a microchip

Many pets have a microchip under the skin, but you can't see it or read it without a scanner. Vets and shelters have scanners. LA Animal Services says every animal admitted to its shelters is scanned, and if a chip is found, staff contact the microchip company to reach the owner. A chip only holds an ID number, so the owner is found through the registry, not the chip itself.

Scanning is usually quick. Call ahead to a nearby vet clinic or your local shelter and ask whether you can bring a found animal in to be scanned.

## 4. Report it to animal services the same day

This is the step people most often skip. Owners search the shelter's records first, so a found report is how they find you. Montgomery County's guidance:

- Either bring the animal to the county Animal Services and Adoption Center, **or**, if you want to keep it at your home, file a found report.
- File online (the county uses Pet Harbor), in person with a photo, or by phone at (240) 773-5900 during open hours.
- The county says many animals are reunited by matching lost reports with found reports.

## 5. Know the rules before keeping it at home

Some places let you care for a found pet at home, but often only with conditions. LA's "Shelter-at-Home" program is one example. LA Animal Services says a lost pet does not need to go to a shelter right away, because pets are often found more easily close to home. But if you keep it, you must:

- notify the nearest LA Animal Services center and send two or more photos;
- within four hours, take the animal to a vet or an LA Animal Services center to be weighed and scanned for a microchip;
- within four hours, make and put up posters with the pet's photo and your contact information where you found it;
- pay for any medical care the animal needs;
- keep the animal at the location you registered for 30 days, and report any changes;
- report it right away if the animal is lost or stolen, and surrender it if the department asks;
- after 30 days but before 32 days, either keep the animal (getting it vaccinated and spayed or neutered, and licensed if it's a dog) or surrender it to an LA Animal Services center.

Other cities and counties may have different time limits or may require you to bring the animal in. Don't assume that keeping a found pet makes it yours. Follow the steps your local agency lists.

## 6. Spread the word nearby

Both example agencies point finders to free lost-and-found pet listings and neighborhood networks. LA Animal Services lists Petco Love Lost (photo matching), PawBoost, its "LA City Lost and Found Pets" page, and Nextdoor. Montgomery County also points to Petco Love Lost. Post a clear photo and say where you found the animal.

When someone contacts you, ask them to describe a detail you left out of the post, or to show a photo or vet record, before you hand the animal over. Many shelters ask owners for this kind of proof (see [What should you do in the first 24 hours after your pet goes missing?](/articles/usa/animals/lost-pet-first-24-hours/)).

## Quick version

1. Stay safe and keep the animal away from your own pets.
2. Check for an ID, rabies, or license tag and call the number.
3. Get it scanned for a microchip at a vet or shelter.
4. File a found report with your local animal services agency the same day.
5. Follow your local rules if you want to keep it at home while you search.
6. Post a photo on the lost-and-found services your agency recommends.
