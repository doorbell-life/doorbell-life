Check for an ID tag and contact the owner if you can, get the animal scanned for a microchip at a shelter or vet, and report it to your local animal services agency. Whether you may keep it at home while you look for the owner depends on local rules.

Found-animal rules are local. This article uses two public agencies as examples, the City of Los Angeles (LA Animal Services) and Montgomery County, Maryland. Your local agency's rules may be different.

## 1. Approach calmly

Montgomery County's advice to owners searching for a lost pet applies here too: even the friendliest pet may become skittish and scared when lost. Don't chase it; the county says you'll only scare it more. Instead, sit on the ground and speak in a soft, friendly, normal voice.

If you take the animal home, LA Animal Services says to keep it safe and separate from your own pets until you can confirm its health and behavior.

## 2. Look for a tag

LA Animal Services says the first step is to check whether the pet has identification tags with contact information, and if it does, to try to contact the owner directly.

A city or county license tag also leads back to the owner. LA Animal Services says each license tag has a unique number, people who find an animal with a tag can call the phone number printed on it, and the department can identify the guardian from the tag number. Montgomery County calls a visible license tag "the fastest way" to be reunited with a lost pet.

## 3. Get it scanned for a microchip

If the pet has no tags or you can't reach the owner, LA Animal Services says to take it to a nearby animal shelter or veterinarian to be scanned for a microchip. A microchip is the size of a grain of rice and sits under the skin; each chip has a unique number registered with a database along with the owner's contact information, and it is read by scanners used by most animal organizations and veterinary clinics.

LA Animal Services says every animal admitted to its shelters is scanned, and if a chip is found, staff contact the microchip company to reach the owner.

## 4. Report it to animal services

- **Montgomery County:** either bring the animal to the county Animal Services and Adoption Center, **or**, if you want to keep it at your home, file a found report: online (Pet Harbor), in person with a photo, or by phone at (240) 773-5900 during open hours. The county says many animals are reunited by matching lost reports with found reports.
- **Los Angeles:** LA Animal Services says to notify local animal shelters and LA Animal Services about the found pet, with a description and where it was found.

## 5. Know the rules before keeping it at home

LA Animal Services says a lost pet does not immediately need to be taken to a shelter, and that lost pets are often found more easily the closer they are to home. Its "Shelter-at-Home" program lets residents care for a found dog or cat that is not wearing a license tag for up to 30 days. If you join, you must:

- within four hours, tell the nearest LA Animal Services center that you have the animal, with a description, where it was found, and where you are keeping it, and send a picture (two poses preferred);
- within four hours, take the animal to a vet or an LA Animal Services center to be weighed and scanned for a microchip;
- within four hours, make and put up posters with the pet's picture and your contact information where you found it;
- pay for any medical care the animal needs;
- keep the animal at the location you registered for 30 days, and report any changes;
- report it right away if the animal is lost or stolen, and surrender it if the department asks;
- after 30 days but before 32 days, either keep the animal (getting it vaccinated and spayed or neutered, and licensed if it's a dog) or surrender it to an LA Animal Services center.

LA Animal Services also warns that if the owner is identified and you fail to release the animal to the shelter, an officer could seize it and you could face theft charges.

## 6. Spread the word nearby

LA Animal Services lists Petco Love Lost (photo matching), PawBoost, its "LA City Lost and Found Pets" page, and Nextdoor for found pets. Montgomery County lists Petco Love Lost among its lost-and-found resources.

Before handing the animal over, you can ask for the kind of proof a shelter uses. Montgomery County asks people reclaiming a pet for at least one of: license and vaccination records, purchase or adoption receipts, microchip registration documents, or veterinary records, with a name that matches their ID. In LA's Shelter-at-Home program, if you return the animal to its owner you must notify LA Animal Services right away and give the owner's name and address.

## Quick version

1. Approach calmly, and keep the animal separate from your own pets.
2. Check for an ID or license tag and contact the owner.
3. Get it scanned for a microchip at a shelter or vet.
4. Report it to your local animal services agency.
5. Follow your local rules if you want to keep it at home while you search.
6. Post it on the lost-and-found services your agency lists.
