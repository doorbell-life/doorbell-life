In the U.S. they are two separate things; you license your pet with your city or county, usually with proof of rabies vaccination, and you register its microchip, if it has one, with the chip's registry company and keep your contact information current there.

Neither the license nor the microchip registry is run by the federal government. Licensing rules are written by local governments and differ a lot from place to place. The three examples below show how they vary; they are not a complete list.

## Part 1: The license (city or county)

### What a license is

A pet license is a registration with your local government. You get a numbered tag for your pet's collar, and the agency keeps a record linking the number to you. LA Animal Services says that if a licensed animal is lost or taken to a shelter, the department can identify the owner from the tag number, and licensed animals are held at the shelter while staff contact the owner. Montgomery County, Maryland, calls a visible license tag "the fastest way" to get a lost pet back.

### Example 1: City of Los Angeles

- All dogs over four months old kept in the city must be licensed (Los Angeles Municipal Code 53.28) and spayed or neutered (LAMC 53.15.2(b)).
- A dog license is valid only with a rabies vaccination certificate from a licensed veterinarian, and only while that vaccination is in effect (LAMC 53.51).
- The license tag must be on the dog's collar or harness whenever it is off your property.
- Fees on the department's page (a schedule dated October 2010, checked November 3, 2026): $20 for one year or $55 for three years for a spayed or neutered dog, and $10 for low-income seniors (62+) and disabled owners. Unaltered dogs fall under the separate spay/neuter rules.
- You can apply or renew online.

### Example 2: Montgomery County, Maryland

- By law, dogs **and cats** over four months old must wear current rabies and pet license tags.
- The rabies tag from your vet is **not** the county license.
- If you live in the City of Rockville or the City of Gaithersburg, you license your pet with your city, not the county.
- The pet's rabies vaccination must be valid for at least 60 more days when you buy the license. License terms follow the vaccine dates, so a "1-year" license can run anywhere from 2 to 12 months.
- You can apply by mail (with a copy of the rabies certificate; the license arrives in about 3 weeks) or in person, which is the only way to get one on the spot. The county lists three ways in all; see its page for the current options.

### Example 3: New York City

- All dogs in New York City must be licensed, and the license must be attached to the collar in public. Owners can be fined.
- Licenses last from one to five years. The cost depends on the length and on whether the dog is spayed or neutered.
- You can apply online or by mail, and the license arrives two to four weeks after payment.
- Trained guard dogs have extra rules, including a required microchip whose number must be given to the Health Department.

### How to find your rule

Search for your city or county's "animal services" or "pet license" page. If you live in an incorporated city inside a county, check both. As Montgomery County's Rockville and Gaithersburg example shows, the city may handle licensing instead of the county.

## Part 2: Microchip registration (the chip's registry)

### What it is

A microchip only holds an ID number. According to the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA, a professional association), you must register the chip and keep your contact information current in the associated registry, or a scanned pet may still not get home. Registration is separate from your city or county license: licensing your pet doesn't register the chip, and registering the chip doesn't license your pet.

The registry is run by the chip's provider, not by the government. Both example agencies send owners to the chip company: Montgomery County tells owners of lost pets to "contact the company who manufactured their chip," and LA Animal Services says to contact your microchip provider to update your details.

### How to check and update it

LA Animal Services lists these steps:

1. Find your pet's chip number. If you don't have it, a vet or shelter can scan your pet.
2. Look the number up with an online microchip lookup tool. The AVMA points to the American Animal Hospital Association's Microchip Registry Lookup Tool, which shows which registry the chip is associated with.
3. Contact that provider and update your information. LA suggests using your mobile number, since people tend to keep it when they move.

The AVMA promotes August 15 as "Check the Chip Day," a yearly reminder to review your registry details.

### Is a microchip required?

Some local rules require one, such as New York City's guard-dog rule above, and the CDC requires a microchip for every dog entering the U.S. We have not verified a complete list of state and local microchip requirements. See [Why should you microchip your pet?](/articles/usa/animals/why-microchip/).

## Quick checklist

- **License:** find your city or county's rule, get a rabies vaccination, buy the license, put the tag on the collar, and renew it before the rabies vaccination or license expires.
- **Microchip:** find the chip number, find the registry, put the chip in your name, and update it whenever your phone number or address changes.
- **Keep both records together** with your vet records. Shelters may ask for license, vaccination, or microchip registration documents as proof of ownership if your pet is ever lost.
