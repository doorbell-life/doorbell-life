At a typical public shelter you browse the animals online or in person, fill out an adoption questionnaire, meet the animal with a counselor, then show ID, pay the fee, and often take your pet home the same day, usually already vaccinated, spayed or neutered, and microchipped; the exact steps are set by each shelter.

In the U.S., public shelters are run by cities and counties, and each sets its own process and fees. This article walks through two of them as examples: the Montgomery County Animal Services and Adoption Center in Maryland, which the county describes as its only open-admission municipal shelter, and LA Animal Services, which runs six shelters for the City of Los Angeles.

## Step 1: Look at who's available

Both shelters post adoptable animals online. Montgomery County says its list updates every two hours and that you're welcome to walk through the shelter to look anytime it's open. Animals include dogs, cats, rabbits, guinea pigs, reptiles, and more.

Montgomery County adopts on a **first-come, first-served** basis. It does not place animals on hold based on a questionnaire, so a particular animal may be gone by the time you arrive. If that happens, staff will try to help you find a similar one.

## Step 2: Fill out the adoption questionnaire

Montgomery County asks every adopter to complete an Adoption Questionnaire about the kind of pet you're looking for. You can fill it out online ahead of time or at the shelter. It's available in English and Spanish, with a printable Chinese version. When you arrive with your documents, staff add you to the wait list for the next available adoption counselor. Wait times depend on demand and staffing, and the last adopter is served one hour before closing.

## Step 3: Bring the right documents

What Montgomery County asks for:

- a government-issued photo ID, such as a driver's license;
- copies of rabies certificates for any dogs, cats, or ferrets already in your household;
- if you live in the county, licenses for your current pets, which you'll have to buy if they aren't already licensed with the county;
- the adoption fee, if one applies.

LA Animal Services also requires a government-issued photo ID (driver's license, state ID, or passport). It takes cash, check, or credit card, and the name on the card must match the ID.

## Step 4: Meet the animal

You meet the animal with a counselor's help. Montgomery County describes a "meet and greet," after which you tell the adoption team whether you'd like to go ahead, and they set up the adoption.

If more than one person wants the same animal, shelters handle it differently. Montgomery County is first-come, first-served. LA Animal Services uses a silent auction among the interested adopters, which it describes as a fair process without favoritism.

## Step 5: Pay the fee and go home

Montgomery County does same-day adoptions: if you fall in love during your appointment, you can take the animal home that day. Bring a carrier for a cat or small animal, or a leash and a collar or harness for a dog (no retractable leashes, the county asks).

## What the fee usually covers

Fees vary by shelter, species, and age, so check your shelter's fee page. What the two example shelters include:

**Montgomery County, dogs:** spay or neuter, microchip, heartworm test (depending on age), distemper, Bordetella, and rabies vaccines, and flea treatment and deworming.

**Montgomery County, cats:** spay or neuter, microchip, distemper and rabies vaccines, and flea treatment and deworming.

**LA Animal Services:** vaccinations, spay or neuter surgery, and a microchip for dogs, cats, and rabbits. The $15 microchip charge is part of the fee and is non-refundable. For City of Los Angeles residents, the fee also covers the first year's dog license. City law requires all dogs and cats over four months old to be spayed or neutered, with some exemptions.

LA Animal Services also explains what happens if an animal you've chosen becomes unable to have surgery because of illness or injury before it leaves the shelter. You'll be notified, and you can take the animal to a private vet for treatment, choose another animal, or ask for a refund (excluding pound, microchip, and optional medical fees). If you go ahead with the adoption, you must take the animal to a private vet right away and pay for its care.

## Fee-waived events

Shelters sometimes waive adoption fees. For example, Montgomery County waived adoption fees on August 29–30, 2026, as part of NBCUniversal Local's 2026 "Clear the Shelters" campaign. Even then, the county noted that licensing fees could still apply for dogs and cats.

## After you get home

- **Register the microchip in your name.** A shelter chip doesn't help if the registry can't reach you. See [Why should you microchip your pet?](/articles/usa/animals/why-microchip/).
- **Get the license your city or county requires.** See [How do pet licensing and microchip registration work in the U.S.?](/articles/usa/animals/pet-registration/).
- **Book a vet visit** and bring the shelter's medical records.

If you haven't adopted yet, start with [What should you prepare before adopting a pet?](/articles/usa/animals/before-adopting/).
