Right away, post a photo and description to neighbors, tell your microchip company, and file a lost report with your local animal services agency; within hours, put up flyers and call nearby vets and shelters; within 24 hours, visit the shelters in person and search your neighborhood.

The timeline below follows the "Lost My Pet" guide from Montgomery County, Maryland's Animal Services and Adoption Center, with tips from LA Animal Services (City of Los Angeles). Report forms and phone numbers differ by agency, so use your local agency's.

## Right away: get the word out

Montgomery County lists four first steps:

1. **Tell your neighbors and community.** Post on Nextdoor, your local lost-and-found pet page, neighborhood email or text lists, and social media. Include a clear, recent photo, a description (breed, color, size, sex), and where your pet was last seen.
2. **Call your microchip company.** If your pet is microchipped, report it lost and confirm that your contact information is current. The American Veterinary Medical Association (a professional association) explains that the chip itself only holds an ID number. If your phone number in the registry is outdated, a shelter that scans your pet may not be able to reach you.
3. **File a lost report with animal services.** Montgomery County takes lost reports online through 24PetConnect. LA Animal Services offers lost-pet registration through 24PetConnect too: it emails you once a day when a matching animal is reported found or arrives at one of its six centers, and the registration lasts 30 days.
4. **Search photo-matching services.** Montgomery County points owners to Petco Love Lost, a free service that compares your pet's photo with photos of found animals from shelters and the public.

If you know your pet's microchip number but not which registry it's in, the AVMA points to the American Animal Hospital Association's Microchip Registry Lookup Tool, or says to ask your veterinarian to help identify the registry. LA Animal Services' microchip page links to online lookup tools as well.

## In the next few hours: flyers and phone calls

Montgomery County's next steps:

- **Make flyers.** Put "LOST DOG" or "LOST CAT" at the top in large, bold letters that can be read from a moving car. Describe your pet instead of relying on the breed name, since many people won't know it.
- **Call vet clinics, including emergency vet hospitals,** in your area and nearby areas. People who pick up a stray sometimes drive it to a clinic far away, especially after hours.
- **Call shelters in neighboring cities and counties.** Pets wander across boundaries, and finders sometimes take them to the wrong shelter.

## Within 24 hours: go out and search

- **Hang flyers at major intersections.** Montgomery County suggests a 3-mile radius from where a dog was lost, and a 1-mile radius for a cat.
- **Visit shelters in person.** Montgomery County asks owners to visit during open hours, ask staff to walk them through, and come back as often as they can, because staff may not recognize your pet from a flyer. LA Animal Services gives the same advice: a scared pet may not photograph well, so don't rely only on online listings. Check every day, and ask to see the hospital ward and isolation areas as well as the regular kennels.
- **Search the neighborhood on foot.** Search where your pet was last seen and places it likes to go. Call its name, and bring treats, a leash, or a carrier. Ask people to call you if they see your pet, and tell them not to chase it.
- **Talk to people who are out every day.** LA Animal Services suggests asking neighbors, letter carriers, and delivery drivers, and checking whether your pet is hiding in a neighbor's garage. It cites an ASPCA survey that found about half of missing dogs and a third of missing cats were found by searching the local neighborhood.

## Have proof of ownership ready

When you find your pet at a shelter, you'll need to show it's yours.

- **LA Animal Services** asks for proof of ownership, such as photos, vaccination records, or adoption certificates, plus a valid government-issued photo ID.
- **Montgomery County** asks for at least one of these: license and vaccination records, purchase or adoption receipts, microchip registration documents, or vet records. The name on them should match your ID.

## Watch out for scams

LA Animal Services warns about "pet recovery scams," where someone demands that you send or wire money before they return your pet. Ask for a photo of your pet first. If they refuse, they may not have it.

## Don't give up after the first day

Montgomery County notes that cats often hide for around 10 days after being displaced, so don't lose hope if no one has seen your cat in the first two weeks. It also says lost pets have been found weeks, months, and even years later, and asks owners to visit the shelter as often as they can. LA Animal Services' lost-pet registration lasts 30 days.

## After your pet is home

Check that your microchip registration and license are current. LA Animal Services says that when a microchipped pet doesn't make it home, it's usually because the owner's information in the registry is wrong or missing.
